Best before: next spring.

A supermarket bag is packed to wait. Months in a warehouse, weeks on a shelf. The date on the back is the day it stops being sellable, not the day it was roasted. Nobody wrote that one down.

Roasted: the date is on the bag.

Coffee is at its best in the first few weeks after roasting. So the roast date goes on every bag, by hand, and nothing leaves the roastery more than a week after it came out of the drum.

Nobody is standing there.

A factory roaster runs all night. It is set once, and it is left.

Very hot, very fast, so the batch is done in minutes and the next one is already loading.

It comes out even, dark, and the same every time. That is the point of it.

Someone is listening.

Ours runs when one of us is standing in front of it, and not otherwise.

A few minutes in there is a sound like kindling catching. First crack. From there every half minute changes the cup, and someone decides, by ear and by nose, when to drop it.

You will taste that decision. It is the whole difference, and it is the only one that matters.
